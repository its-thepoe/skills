# An evidence-backed working model

This is a synthesis of public evidence, not a framework published by Ive. Source
IDs resolve in [sources.md](sources.md). Statements about his beliefs are distinct
from this skill's practical applications. Read the relevant [cases](cases.md) for
context before transferring a historical choice to a new situation.

## 1. The way the problem is described is already a design decision

**Evidence:** McKinsey (A20) connects extensive writing to qualities drawings
cannot express and to avoiding premature exclusions. The lunch exercise (B02)
demonstrates the consequence of assuming the container must be a box. The 2012
interview (A04) distinguishes irritation-led problem solving from pursuing an
unarticulated opportunity.

**Application:** Write a brief in terms of what someone should be able to do or
experience. Then write two competing versions: one addressing current friction,
another questioning the category or mechanism. Identify nouns that smuggle in a
solution. Do not endlessly reframe a clear, urgent usability defect.

**Useful question:** What have we decided simply by naming it this way?

## 2. Simplicity must survive use

**Evidence:** Dazed (A12) distinguishes a minimal appearance from simplicity in
the underlying architecture. The G5 account (A03) makes it tangible in removable
components and understandable access. Ferrari (A24) starts from interaction
organisation, rather than treating a touchscreen as the obvious solution.

**Application:** Count the decisions, dependencies, mode switches, and things a
person must remember. Follow complexity when a control disappears: did the team
eliminate a need, absorb work into the system, or merely transfer it to the user?
Remove redundant states and concepts before removing useful signifiers.

**Useful question:** Is this simpler to live with, or only simpler to photograph?

## 3. Make the important uncertainty tangible

**Evidence:** The 2014 conversation (A08) describes models making tentative ideas
shareable. The 2003 interview (A02) describes cycles of sketching and physical
prototypes. Parker's reporting (A09) records carrying phone-size models in daily
life; it also warns that seductive execution can conceal a weak idea.

**Application:** Choose fidelity by the question. A paper flow can test sequence;
a working transition can test interruption and recovery; a weighted mock-up can
test grip. A render cannot prove balance or repairability. Compare alternatives
under the same conditions and revisit an initially exciting idea after use.

**Useful question:** What must we make before this conversation becomes reliable?

## 4. Form, interaction, engineering, and production belong in the same conversation

**Evidence:** ICON (A02) describes collaboration before technical architecture is
fixed. Dazed (A12) discusses resolving phone interaction with hardware and software
together. MacStories' excerpt (D18) rejects treating making as an afterthought.

**Application:** Map the human experience to the mechanisms enabling it. Bring
engineering, operations, fabrication, and support into a consequential decision
early. In software, this includes latency, data structure, permissions, offline
behaviour, and recovery. These are modern translations of the principle, not
documented prescriptions by Ive for a particular stack.

**Useful question:** Which apparent styling problem is actually a system problem?

## 5. Care is experienced through encounters, including overlooked ones

**Evidence:** The Design Museum Q&A (A01) discusses handles, cables, adaptors, and
the accessibility of objects. Stripe (A21) uses unwrapping a cable to discuss
feeling considered. These are his beliefs about how care is perceived, not
empirical proof that every expensive detail improves experience.

**Application:** Walk through arrival, first contact, normal use, mistakes,
cleaning, maintenance, and departure. Look for the moment a person feels the
product anticipated their situation. In software, examine first-use guidance,
empty states, failed saves, lost work, cancellation, and data export.

**Useful question:** Where does a real person currently have to compensate for us?

## 6. An idea needs protection and an honest test

**Evidence:** McKinsey (A20) explains the asymmetry between visible obstacles and
unproven potential. Hirshhorn reporting (A15) emphasises listening and trust. In
the 2017 conversation (A14), focus and shared curiosity are recurring concerns.

**Application:** Separate generative discussion from selection. For a fragile
proposal, state what it could enable, then turn objections into falsifiable
questions. Set an exploration budget and an exit condition. Protect a promising
mechanism from premature aesthetic judgement; do not shield a shipped failure
from evidence or a powerful person from disagreement.

**Useful question:** Are we identifying a reason to stop, or work we must do?

## 7. Better is a meaningful change in experience

**Evidence:** The Standard (A04) rejects novelty as the goal. The Design Museum
(A01) places use ahead of superficial differentiation. The Ferrari discussion
(A24) pairs cross-domain curiosity with deference to people who understand making.

**Application:** Complete: “This is better for ___ because they can now ___.”
Compare against the existing product, including the burden of relearning or
migration. If a recommendation only makes it more fashionable or more recognisably
the designer's, challenge it. Retain a strong existing solution where appropriate.

**Useful question:** What becomes better if nobody notices that we redesigned it?

## 8. History supplies mechanisms and meaning, not a costume

**Evidence:** LoveFrom Serif's team interview (B13) investigates Baskerville's
source material and extends it for contemporary use. Christie's (A25) adapts a
historic rostrum to current auctioneering and livestream audiences. The Rams
Foundation interview (A28) values broadly available production as well as form.

**Application:** Study what earlier solutions enabled, the circumstances that
shaped them, and which constraints have changed. Borrow a useful relationship,
gesture, or manufacturing insight. Do not assume historical appearance is honest
or suitable for a modern function. Credit the specialists who did the work.

**Useful question:** What did the old solution understand that we are overlooking?

**Cultural check:** A17 explicitly describes perception as shaped by historical
and cultural references. Do not treat your familiarity, taste, or interpretation
as universal. Test the meaning with the actual audience.

## 9. Coherence allows expressiveness

**Evidence:** The Terra Carta work (B09/B16) is intricate; the serif is expressive;
the Moncler systems (B18/A26) include playful identity. Stripe (A21) discusses joy
and questions the opposition between utility and beauty. These projects challenge
the claim that Ive's enduring method is a fixed minimalist style.

**Application:** Establish a clear hierarchy of purpose. Use colour, motion,
texture, typography, or ornament when it improves comprehension, identity, warmth,
or the experience of use. Ask what it contributes and what it demands in return.
Do not strip a game, cultural institution, or data tool of its particular nature.

**Useful question:** Does this detail belong to the thing, or advertise its maker?

## 10. A team and a product should accumulate learning

**Evidence:** WIRED25 (A16) places responsibility beyond shipping. McKinsey (A20)
values designing later generations with prior learning. Moncler (B18) and
Christie's (A25) return to what collaborators learned as an outcome of making.

**Application:** Keep a decision record: question, alternatives, evidence,
rejection reason, uncertainty, and what would reopen the choice. Read field and
support evidence before proposing the next version. Name both an improvement to
the product and a capability the team gains. Avoid copying a famous team's exact
rituals without asking what your team needs.

**Useful question:** What can the next version know that this one did not?

## The counterweights are part of the model

### Thinness, enclosure, and longevity

iFixit's legacy critique (D08) challenges sealed, difficult-to-service products.
Its keyboard analysis (D09) shows why tolerance of ordinary contamination and
repair consequences matter. These are critical accounts of products, not evidence
that Ive personally specified each failure mechanism.

**Decision rule for this skill:** Expose the lifecycle cost of elegance. Compare
reliability, access, battery replacement, service time, replaceable wear parts,
and the cost of failure. In software, compare recoverability, portability,
maintainability, and support effort. Avoid declaring a single lifecycle metric
universally decisive; make the conflict and its beneficiaries explicit.

### Creative authority and user evidence

His resistance to focus groups in A04 concerns asking users to invent tomorrow's
product. It does not establish that evaluation is unnecessary. The reported
carrying of prototypes and the stress-testing described in A24 support testing
assumptions. Ask users to demonstrate present behaviour, not design the answer
for you. Bring accessibility and domain expertise to the evaluation.

### Craft and opportunity cost

A tiny detail may unlock the whole product; it may also absorb time better spent
on a severe defect. State its contribution, who experiences it, and how one would
recognise diminishing returns. A small team's budget does not have to reproduce
Apple's fabrication resources to apply careful judgement.

### Sources are situated

First-person interviews reveal intentions. Product films are edited persuasion.
Client announcements establish commissioned work, not independent effectiveness.
Studio reporting provides observation but remains selective. Books by colleagues
describe their viewpoint; Jobs's views and Rams's principles are not automatically
Ive's. Reviews can expose consequences without establishing a designer's motive.

## Calibrate the conclusion

| Claim | Appropriate strength |
| --- | --- |
| Repeated direct statements plus observed practice | “A well-supported principle is…” |
| A documented choice in one product | “In this case, the team chose…” |
| Transfer to the user's design | “An Ive-informed application would be…” |
| Predicted response to an unseen or untested experience | “This may…; test it by…” |
| Unread, metadata-only, or unresolved source | Research lead only; do not use as proof |
