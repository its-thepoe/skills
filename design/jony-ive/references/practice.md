# Applying the lens

These exercises operationalise the [principles](principles.md). They are original
methods for using this skill, not a reconstruction of a secret Apple process or a
mandatory sequence that Ive followed on every project.

## Critique: find the consequential friction

1. **Describe the intention:** “A person needs to ___ in ___ conditions, with
   confidence that ___.” Mark missing context.
2. **Walk the experience:** approach → understand → begin → act → get feedback →
   recover → finish → own or return. Skip stages irrelevant to the artefact.
3. **Name the dominant contradiction:** for example, a calm visual surface whose
   operation requires memorisation; a modular promise with a fragile connector;
   a friendly service with a punitive cancellation path.
4. **Locate its cause:** test information structure, state model, assembly, or
   organisational handoff before treating the symptom as styling.
5. **Recommend the smallest coherent intervention:** preserve strengths, fix the
   governing relationship, then refine details that support it.

For each finding use this compact structure:

> **Change:** the actual intervention and the part it affects. **Why:** evidence
> and human consequence. **Basis:** the relevant principle and source. **Cost:**
> what it complicates or sacrifices. **Test:** the observation that would support
> or reverse the decision.

Avoid fabricated numerical precision. Use a proposed target only if identified as
a target; derive it from the user's context where possible. Do not invent pixel
measurements from an uncalibrated image or claim users are confused without data.

## Compare: choose the strongest whole

Write the intended experience and non-negotiables before evaluating the options.
Assess the actual options; do not reward the one that resembles an Apple product.

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Human task | Which option most directly enables the primary outcome? |
| Comprehension | What must be learned, inferred, or remembered? |
| Interaction | Does the input/output relationship suit the body and situation? |
| Coherence | Do the parts express one understandable organising idea? |
| Feasibility | What must engineering, operations, or production make true? |
| Care | What happens in the overlooked and failure states? |
| Ownership | Who pays for maintenance, recovery, replacement, or migration? |
| Character | Does its expression serve this product and its people? |

Use **supported / uncertain / fails a requirement** with evidence. Avoid arbitrary
weighted scores that pretend to measure taste. If weights are useful, agree them
from the task and expose sensitivity to a different weighting.

Recommend one option when evidence permits. If both fail the primary promise,
say so and describe a third structural direction. If an unknown could reverse the
choice, provide a provisional preference and the smallest deciding test.

## Reframe: write a more fertile brief

Produce three short versions, not a long discovery questionnaire:

- **Experienced problem:** what is difficult today, in the person's own context?
- **Underlying need:** what capability or reassurance is missing?
- **Opportunity:** what would become possible if a familiar mechanism were absent?

Then choose a brief. Include desired qualities beyond appearance, real constraints,
what the product is responsible for, and what evidence would count as improvement.
Keep aspiration and verified need separate. A new opportunity need not have a
pre-existing feature request, but it still needs a credible human benefit.

Example: “Make the reservation page premium” becomes “Help a guest choose a suitable
time and understand the commitment before booking.” Possible directions concern
availability, reassurance, and flexibility before palette or imagery.

## Develop: create alternatives that differ in principle

Start with a concise written intention. Offer two or three concepts with distinct
mechanisms, not the same layout in different colours. For each provide:

1. Organising idea and primary human benefit.
2. How the person acts and how the system responds.
3. What has been removed, combined, made explicit, or newly enabled.
4. The hard problem engineering or operations must solve.
5. The main risk, affected user, and what the prototype must settle.

Choose a preferred direction and explain why. Treat a promising but immature
concept as a question to explore. Do not manufacture certainty by presenting
polished renderings of an unresolved mechanism.

### Select a prototype by what you must learn

| Uncertainty | Credible early prototype | Observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding a promise | Short written scenario and rough storyboard | Can someone explain what changes for them? |
| Task sequence / information | Paper or clickable flow with realistic content | Starts, wrong turns, completion, recovery |
| Timing / transitions | Small working slice on the target device | Feedback, latency, interruption, reduced motion |
| Scale / reach / grip | Full-size mock-up, weighted if necessary | Posture, reach, fatigue, accidental activation |
| Material / assembly | Samples, joints, or a mechanism rig | Temperature, sound, force, wear, repair access |
| Service handoff | Rehearsal with staff and representative users | Waits, responsibility gaps, exceptions |
| Ownership | Repair/recovery and support walkthrough | Downtime, cost, lost work, ability to leave |

A test plan is incomplete without a decision: “Keep this if ___; change or reject
it if ___.” Pilot with a few representative participants to discover issues; do
not generalise small-sample impressions into population-level claims.

## Studio: build conditions for good work

Base the approach on listening, trust, shared artefacts, and accumulated learning
(A15/A20/A21). Adapt it to team size, geography, resources, and preferences.

### A useful review session

1. Send a short intention and the open decision before the meeting.
2. Let people inspect the same model or working example in silence first.
3. Ask for clarifying questions and a fair account of the proposal's promise.
4. Discuss observations, then interpretations, then alternatives. Make room for
   quieter contributions, including written input.
5. Invite the relevant engineer, maker, operator, or support specialist when the
   decision depends on their knowledge.
6. Record the decision, unresolved assumption, owner, next artefact, and revisit
   condition. End when these are clear, not when every participant has an opinion.

For generative work, defer selection until the idea is understood. For a release
review, hold it to the intended quality and known constraints. Do not use one
session's temperament for both stages.

The commencement account reported in D19 offers a useful ritual: make room to
present the work and explain it thoughtfully, even in messy or inconvenient
circumstances. The purpose is attention and respect, not ceremony, an expensive
studio, or insulation from constructive criticism.

### Learning record

Use this when the user asks for a research notebook or durable decision log:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Question | The decision and why it matters |
| Principle | Documented belief; source ID and exact section if checked |
| Behaviour / artefact | What was observed, made, or tested |
| Reasoning | Why the team chose it; distinguish inference |
| Constraint | The actual limit, including whose limit it is |
| Trade-off | Benefit and burden, with affected people |
| Confidence | Direct statement / observed / reported / interpretation |
| Test and result | Evidence that supports or challenges the choice |
| Next version | Learning to carry forward; what would reopen the decision |

## Domain translation

For **software**, inspect state, content, response, errors, permissions, assistive
technology, and maintainability. Do not claim software should behave like milled
metal. For **physical objects**, require handling and mechanism evidence before
judging ergonomics or material suitability. For **services**, inspect time, staff,
handoffs, dignity, cancellation, and recovery. For **identity**, examine the cultural
meaning, applications, reproduction, and legibility. For **spaces**, follow bodies,
sightlines, accessibility, sound, climate, maintenance, and public consequences.

The shared concern is a coherent, considerate experience. The implementation and
the evidence needed remain specific to each domain.
