# Product decisions as working precedents

Read a case for the mechanism relevant to the user's problem. The transfer and
experiment sections are this skill's proposals, not historical instructions from
Ive. Source IDs resolve in [sources.md](sources.md).

## iMac: approachability can be an affordance

**Evidence:** A01 explains the handle as both a way to move the computer and an
invitation to touch it. The material and colour are part of its specific context.

**Transfer:** A first-time user's confidence can depend on an unambiguous place to
begin. An honest labelled action or a welcoming empty state may do more than an
extra paragraph about ease of use. Do not add a decorative handle or familiar
metaphor that lies about what the thing does.

**Experiment:** Put the product in front of a first-time user without narration.
Observe their first approach and whether they handle or operate it as intended.

## Power Mac G5: the inside can simplify the outside

**Evidence:** A03 describes accessible components, fans connecting without loose
wiring, and instructions inside the access panel. Reduction concerns construction
and service as well as appearance.

**Transfer:** Before hiding an interface, ask whether the underlying relationships
can become more direct. A coherent state model can eliminate redundant settings.
For an object, fewer connectors may improve assembly, but test service access.

**Experiment:** Compare assembly or task walkthroughs, including a common repair
or recovery. Note what must be explained, remembered, or taken apart.

## Unibody: production can change what the object can be

**Evidence:** Supplement S01 documents Apple's 2008 move to an enclosure machined
from a single aluminium block. This corroborates the process theme even though
the supplied Hardwareluxx reupload (C03) remains unresolved.

**Transfer:** Investigate the process enabling a quality, rather than specifying a
surface imitation. A shared software primitive or different assembly strategy may
produce consistency more reliably than manually correcting every instance.

**Cost:** Tooling, yield, material use, repair, and budget belong in the comparison.
Machining a monolithic enclosure is not a universal design recommendation.

## iPhone 5: precision needs a human purpose

**Evidence boundary:** C04's design film is located through contemporary reporting;
the full film was not reviewed for this package. Do not cite a particular tolerance
or manufacturing technique from that film without checking it.

**Transfer:** Define which physical or digital seam someone encounters: a sharp
edge, a misaligned assembly, a layout jump, a focus loss, or a delayed response.
Test that seam. Precision is valuable when it makes handling and operation better;
numbers in a launch film alone do not establish that value.

## Larger iPhones: a feature is not yet a product

**Evidence:** A08 discusses balancing a larger display with the whole object.
Parker's account A09 records evaluating size models outside the studio.

**Transfer:** Evaluate a feature in context: a larger panel, richer dashboard, or
new control may improve one task while harming reach, attention, portability, or
another task. Do not let the feature's appeal substitute for evaluating the whole.

**Experiment:** Compare the candidate against the existing product during repeated
ordinary tasks, not only the demo task designed to make the new feature shine.

## Apple Watch: invent the interaction for the scale

**Evidence:** B05 documents the Digital Crown for scrolling and zooming. A09 links
it to the limits of interacting directly with a small screen.

**Transfer:** A control suited to one body, attention budget, and surface may fail
at another scale. Study occlusion, reach, accidental activation, tactile feedback,
and the meaning inherited from the familiar control. Do not transplant a phone
interface unchanged to a wearable, car, or kiosk.

**Experiment:** Operate the primary task on the actual intended surface with the
relevant hand position, posture, and interruption conditions.

## iOS 7: a design rationale is a hypothesis to test

**Evidence:** B04 describes layers, typography, translucency, and motion as part of
Apple's redesign rationale. It is a first-party announcement.

**Transfer:** Use hierarchy and feedback to explain relationships. Treat legibility,
contrast, touch targets, orientation, and reduced-motion needs as product
requirements. A published intention to improve clarity does not prove it did.

**Experiment:** Compare navigation and recovery in ordinary and adverse conditions.
Inspect text scaling and motion settings before calling the new visual system
clearer. Do not assume low contrast or thin type follows from Ive's principles.

## LoveFrom Serif: optical judgement and historical research

**Evidence:** B13 describes studying Baskerville's punches and printed material,
then developing new weights, optical adjustments, and curves with specialist
designers. B14's accessible passages establish the studio's sustained investment.

**Transfer:** A mathematically regular system can still look or read poorly.
Evaluate the real content, sizes, rendering, and medium. Invite specialist
judgement where the detail exceeds your competence. Research history to understand
the problem; do not make every brand use a Baskerville-like serif.

**Experiment:** Test actual names, numbers, dense passages, and difficult character
combinations at production size. Review the relationships before isolated glyphs.

## The Apple design book: the medium has its own requirements

**Evidence:** A13 explains that accurately showing the physical products required
attention to paper, ink, photography, and packaging. The archive also let the team
study its learning across products. Calling the account objective is the team's
aspiration, not a guarantee that selection is neutral.

**Transfer:** A representation is a designed artefact too. If a prototype, report,
or specification conceals the quality you need to judge, change the medium.
Beautiful presentation should reveal the question, not conceal an unresolved one.

**Experiment:** Compare what someone can correctly assess from the representation
with what they discover in the real product. Repair consequential gaps before
making the presentation more polished.

## Moncler: simplify a system through its connection

**Evidence:** A26's accessible text describes a modular core with alternative
outer layers and a magnetic fastener. S02 corroborates the 2024 system's material,
fastener development, and packaging. B18 emphasises learning across disciplines.

**Transfer:** Find the interface between parts: a fastening, a data handoff, an
integration setup, or switching service tiers. A better connection can simplify
the whole without making every component identical.

**Experiment:** Test connection, disconnection, wrong orientation, wear, and
maintenance. For software, test partial completion, interrupted transfer, and
recovery. Include the setup instructions and packaging in the experience.

## The RED auction: expression can reveal a change of state

**Evidence:** A05 reports Ive and Newson studying manufacture and choosing red
inside a piano to emphasise its transformation when opened for performance.
The colour was tied to the object and occasion, not merely applied as a signature.

**Transfer:** A meaningful accent can reveal a state, action, or relationship.
For an interface, test whether the accent conveys a useful distinction and has
an accessible equivalent. For an object, evaluate its effect in the actual setting.
The choice of red in this commission is not a universal palette recommendation.

## Ferrari Luce: hierarchy before a preferred interface technology

**Evidence:** A24 explains organising input and output around driving, verifying
assumptions with engineers, and favouring tangible controls. It is a report of
the team's rationale, not independent evidence of comparative driving safety.

**Transfer:** Select an input method by the task, body, context, feedback, and
attention available. Do not infer a universal ban on touchscreens or a requirement
to add physical knobs to every product.

**Experiment:** Compare control identification and operation under representative
conditions, with appropriate domain and safety expertise. Screen polish cannot
establish that an interaction is safe while driving.

## Christie's rostrum: the whole scene is the product

**Evidence:** A25 connects historical research to an object viewed in the round
and through livestreams. It describes collaboration with Benchmark, auctioneer
behaviour, material, and the development of visible wear.

**Transfer:** Include the performer, audience, camera, surrounding space, repeated
gesture, maintenance, and aging. A screen or service may serve people who never
touch the principal control. Design for their perspective as well.

**Experiment:** Rehearse the complete event in context. Observe entry, work,
visibility, sound, exit, and subsequent cleaning or handoff.

## Linn LP12-50: respect what already works

**Evidence:** B17's accessible account describes a deliberately modest collaboration
with an established, modular turntable. It supports respectful evolution; the
complete feature is not available in the bundled research.

**Transfer:** Ask whether a small, well-chosen change serves the user better than
a new visual language or architecture. Test with existing users, preserving their
valuable habits and the product's compatibility where relevant.

## Vogue libellula: motion must be tested in the world

**Evidence:** A27 reports paper objects developed with prototypes, simulation,
and physical trials in a hangar and the venue. It combines construction and the
effect of many objects moving through a space.

**Transfer:** Evaluate a dynamic effect over time and at the intended scale. A
beautiful single frame is insufficient. Combine simulation with a real trial
when geometry, physics, performance, or human attention can invalidate it.

**Experiment:** Test the transition, the aggregate effect, and the aftermath. In
software this includes actual device performance, interruption, and reduced motion.

## Balmuda Sailing Lantern: a verb can open a behaviour

**Evidence:** B21 describes considering a light's ending as extinguishing rather
than merely switching off. The account relates language to the mechanism, sound,
and fading light, alongside use at sea and expected surface wear.

**Transfer:** Describe what an action should feel like over time before choosing
an animation. “Settle,” “release,” or “return” may invite different feedback than
“close.” This is exploration, not permission to slow an urgent action or obscure
whether it completed. Material character must suit the ownership conditions.

**Experiment:** Test the complete sequence on a working mechanism or target device.
Observe clarity of completion, interruption, and repeated use. For the physical
object, independently verify environmental performance instead of trusting a story.

## Repair and the butterfly keyboard: include ordinary failure

**Evidence:** D09 analyses contamination and repair implications of butterfly
keyboards; D08 challenges difficult-to-repair products more broadly.

**Transfer:** A design's cleanest state is only one state. Include dust, wear,
imperfect handling, lost access, broken dependencies, and the economics of repair.
Attribute criticism to the product and its documented outcomes, without inventing
which individual caused a specific engineering decision.

**Experiment:** Define the expected conditions of ownership, then test the most
plausible failure and how a person recovers. Expose any gain in appearance that
requires the user to tolerate an unacceptable failure burden.
